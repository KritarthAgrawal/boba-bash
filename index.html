The Evolution
of Earth

Let me tell you how I began. Before I had a name, I was a cloud of dust and gas, drifting in the dark.

Come along. It’s a long story, but I’ll keep it moving.

4.5 billion years ago

I was not a gentle place

I was a ball of glowing rock, and the sky kept throwing things at me. One crash was so big it tore a piece of me away. That piece became the Moon.

Then, slowly, I cooled. Steam turned to rain, and the rain fell for a very long time. When it stopped, I had oceans.

The Moon is still close by, tugging at my tides.

3.8 billion years ago

Something small woke up

Deep in the water, a few chemicals learned to copy themselves. Nobody planned it. It was tiny, quiet and easy to miss.

But it held on, and it changed. For three billion years that was all the life I had: invisible, patient and busy.

All of it was too small to see without a microscope.

2.4 billion years ago

The day the sky changed

Some of those tiny cells learned to live on sunlight. Their waste was a gas called oxygen, and they made a lot of it.

It wiped out many of my oldest residents. It was also the best thing that ever happened to me, because it made room for bigger lives.

Nearly every breath you take began with their work.

540 to 66 million years ago

Things with eyes and legs

Then life got ambitious. Shells, bones, eyes. Creatures crept out of the water onto my shores, and forests followed. For over 150 million years, dinosaurs walked on me.

Then a rock from space ended their reign within a few short years. The small furry survivors inherited my land.

The birds outside your window are dinosaur family.

About 300,000 years ago

Then you arrived

A new kind of animal looked up at my night sky and wondered. It learned to speak, to use fire, to make tools and to tell stories.

It walked across every continent I had. I didn’t know it yet, but I was getting company.

Stories let each generation start where the last one stopped.

About 12,000 years ago

You planted a seed

People stopped following their food and started growing it. Villages appeared, then towns, then the first cities.

You invented writing so memories wouldn’t die with people. Trade, laws and art spread from place to place.

Farming made almost everything after it possible.

1760 to today

You got fast

In the last 250 years I’ve watched you build engines, light up the night and send voices across oceans. Then came computers and satellites.

Now there are over 8 billion of you. I’m proud of you, and a little worried.

From space, your cities glow at night.

Tomorrow

What happens next is up to you

I’ve been through fire, ice and falling rocks, and I’m still here. But I don’t know how this chapter ends. That part you write.

Look after the forests, the water and each other. I’ll keep turning.

The next page is blank.

Thanks for listening. I’m about 4.5 billion years old, and my story isn’t finished.
